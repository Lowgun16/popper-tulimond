**WHO MADE THIS**

You should know who made your shirt. It tells you whether to trust it.

I never went to fashion school. I think that's the most important thing I can tell you.

I played professional baseball. I pushed my body past what it was built to take, and then past that. I came up in a world where competition, hard work, accountability, and sacrifice for people other than yourself weren't ideals you admired from a distance — they were the air. That world made me. It's the only world I know how to make anything for.

Somewhere along the way I went looking for a shirt that would let a capable man look like what he already was — without shouting, without a logo, without trying too hard. I couldn't find it. The people making clothes for men had quit on them a long time ago. So I made the one I'd been looking for.

I'm not a designer dressing a man he's only read about. I'm the man it's for. I know what it is to carry weight no one sees, to put yourself last, to let the work speak and expect no ceremony for it. I didn't study that. I lived it. The shirt comes from the same place.

So here's the part I want you to understand. I didn't name this after myself. I could have. I didn't, because it was never going to be mine. It belongs to the men who wear it. If you put it on and it's wrong, that's on me — Logan Sorensen. If you put it on and it's everything it should be, that's Popper Tulimond. That's the team's. I just lead it.

That's the whole code, really. A man steps up, takes the blame, and hands the credit to the people he's carrying.

Now you know who made your shirt.

— Logan Sorensen
*for Popper Tulimond*
